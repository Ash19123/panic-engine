Who reaches safety in time?

PANIC ENGINE simulates a crowd evacuating a real campus road map after a warning. It tests how routing and warning time change the outcome.

The problem

An alert helps only if people can act on it in the time they have.

When an alert goes out, everyone is told to go to a safe place at the same moment. They all pick the nearest exits and the same roads, so queues form and some people run out of time before the strike.

How should people be routed, and how much warning time is needed, so that most reach safety in time?

1 · ALERTEveryone receives the warning at once.
2 · SAME ROADSEach person takes the shortest path to the nearest safe stop.
3 · QUEUESRoads fill, travel slows, and time runs out.

How it works

Five parts, built in this order. Each one is simple on its own.

A real road network

Roads come from OpenStreetMap. Each road has a length, a walking time and a capacity. A labelled synthetic grid is the fallback.

Agents with a life cycle

Each person is waiting, moving, queued, evacuated or unable to evacuate. A seed makes every run reproducible.

Routing that sees crowds

Dijkstra finds paths. The congestion-aware version adds the slowdown from crowding, so people spread over more roads and safe stops.

A bomb scenario

A blast radius, an alert radius, an alert time and a strike time. At the strike, roads and safe stops inside the blast are lost.

Everything stored in SQL

Every run is saved in SQLite and analysed with SQL: joins, window functions and integrity checks.

Inspiration

Carles, Chapuis, Douvinet, Péroche and Bopp (2024)

“An Agent-Based Model to Simulate the Effects of Tsunami Warnings on Pedestrian Evacuation: Sensitivity Analysis and Early Findings.” PRIMA 2024, pp. 369–384. DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-77367-9_28. HAL hal-04794796.

They simulate tsunami warnings in Cannes and study alert timing and whether pedestrians reach safety in time. We took inspiration from these questions. PANIC ENGINE is our own model for a bomb scenario, not a replication of their work.

Limits and ethics

  • This is an educational model. It is not a real alert system and cannot send a message to anyone.
  • It is not validated against real evacuations. Speeds, capacities and delays are assumptions.
  • Safe stops are hypothetical, not official shelters.
  • The blast is a radius rule only. People still inside it at the strike are counted as simulated casualties. These are counts in a model, not predictions of real harm.
